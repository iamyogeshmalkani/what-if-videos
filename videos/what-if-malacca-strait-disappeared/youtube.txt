TITLE
What if the Strait of Malacca Disappeared? 🚢

DESCRIPTION
What if the world's busiest shipping shortcuts suddenly disappeared?

Meet the Strait of Malacca, the narrow route between the Malay Peninsula and the Indonesian island of Sumatra. It connects the Indian Ocean with the South China Sea and the Pacific, and millions of barrels of oil pass through it every day. If it were blocked, ships would need longer routes, which would raise travel time and shipping costs, from fuel to electronics.

📌 Quick facts
• The Strait of Malacca lies between the Malay Peninsula and Sumatra
• It links the Indian Ocean with the South China Sea and the Pacific
• Ships between the Middle East and East Asia depend heavily on it
• The alternative routes shown (round Sumatra, through the Sunda Strait) are an illustration
Figures are approximate and rounded.
